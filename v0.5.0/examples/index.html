Examples

Each section builds one kind of orbit and reads off what is particular to it. The notebook example/KerrGeodesics_56_Orbit_Catalog.ipynb holds one set of constants for each of the 56 cases, with plots and the animated gallery shown on the home page.

A stable orbit

kg = kerr_geodesic(0.9, 10.0, 0.5, 0.8)
stable = kg.Stable
(stable.CaseId, stable.Trajectory.r(0.0), stable.Trajectory.z(0.0))
(:A1, 6.666666666666667, 0.6)

With zero initial phases, $\lambda = 0$ is at periapsis, $r = p/(1 + e)$, and at the northern polar turning point. initPhases moves the start along the orbit:

shifted = kerr_geodesic(0.9, 10.0, 0.5, 0.8; initPhases = (0.0, π, 0.0, 0.0)).Stable
shifted.Trajectory.r(0.0)                       # apoapsis, p/(1 - e)
20.0

A circular equatorial orbit has $e = 0$ and $x = \pm1$:

circular = kerr_geodesic(0.9, 8.0, 0.0, 1.0).Stable
(circular.CaseId, circular.Trajectory.r(3.0), circular.Trajectory.theta(3.0))
(:A2, 7.999999999999995, 1.5707963267948966)

Critical orbits

These constants have an unstable spherical orbit at $a = 0.7$. The family holds its three Critical members, ordered by role:

kg = kerr_geodesic(0.7, (0.9171300256198305, 2.2591913519439517, 2.898994491984013))
[(member.CaseId, member.Role, member.Status.name) for member in kg.Critical]
3-element Vector{Tuple{Symbol, Symbol, Symbol}}:
 (:K3, :on_root, :unstable_spherical)
 (:K4, :outer, :homoclinic)
 (:K5, :inner, :whirling)

The homoclinic orbit K4 leaves the spherical orbit, turns at apoapsis at $\lambda = 0$ and comes back, so every radius off the root is reached twice. lambda_of_radius takes the branch:

homoclinic = kg.Critical[2]
r1 = homoclinic.Trajectory.r(1.0)
(homoclinic.Trajectory.r(0.0),
 homoclinic.Trajectory.lambda_of_radius(r1; branch = :incoming),
 homoclinic.Trajectory.lambda_of_radius(r1; branch = :outgoing))
(5.992222372391139, 0.9999999999999996, -0.9999999999999996)

The whirling orbit K5 leaves the spherical orbit inward and crosses the horizon at $\lambda = 0$, where $v$ vanishes:

whirl = kg.Critical[3]
(whirl.Domain.mino, whirl.Trajectory.v(0.0))
((-Inf, 0.0), 0.0)

The ISCO or ISSO is the Critical member K1, at $p =$ kerr_geo_isso(a, x) and $e = 0$:

isso = kerr_geo_isso(0.9, 0.8)
kerr_geodesic(0.9, isso, 0.0, 0.8).Critical[1].CaseId
:K1

A single Critical member can also be built on its own:

from_infinity = kerr_geo_critical_component(0.7, 1.0, -0.7, 16.0; case_id = :K7)
from_infinity.Domain.endpoint_roles
(:past_infinity, :future_repeated_root_asymptote)

A plunge

plunge = kerr_geodesic(0.9, (0.94, 0.1, 12.0)).Plunge
λH = plunge.Domain.horizon_lambda
(plunge.CaseId, plunge.Trajectory.r(0.0), plunge.Trajectory.r(λH))
(:B4, 3.2039552906913054, 1.4358898943540672)

The plunge starts at its turning point and crosses the horizon at $\lambda_H$, where $v$ and $\psi$ vanish and Boyer–Lindquist $t$ diverges:

(plunge.Trajectory.v(λH), plunge.Trajectory.psi(λH), plunge.Trajectory.t(0.999λH))
(0.0, 0.0, 40.13577466775028)

A capture

capture = kerr_geo_capture_component(0.9, 1.1, 0.5, 3.0)
(capture.CaseId, capture.Domain.mino)
(:C3, (-0.8428413119588916, 0.0))

The capture comes in from infinity at $\lambda_\infty < 0$ and crosses the horizon at $\lambda = 0$. $t$ and $\phi$ vanish halfway in Mino time, at ReferenceZero.t_phi_zero_lambda:

(capture.ReferenceZero.t_phi_zero_lambda, capture.ReferenceZero.t_phi_zero_radius,
 capture.Trajectory.v(0.0))
(-0.4214206559794458, 3.7260136141235596, 0.0)

With four complex radial roots and $Q < 0$ the orbit is C5, and its polar motion is vortical: it stays in one hemisphere.

vortical = kerr_geo_capture_four_complex(0.9, 1.8, 0.2, -1.0; polar_hemisphere = :north)
λmid = vortical.Domain.mino[1] / 2
(vortical.CaseId, vortical.Status.polar.sector, vortical.Trajectory.z(λmid))
(:C5, :vortical, 0.9619846157975651)

With $L_z = 0$ and $a^2(1 - E^2) < Q < 0$, the same constants can also describe motion over the poles, and polar_sector chooses it:

a, E = 0.9, 1.5
over_the_poles = kerr_geo_capture_four_complex(a, E, 0.0, a^2 * (1 - E^2) + 1e-5;
                                               polar_sector = :axis_crossing)
over_the_poles.Status.polar.sector
:axis_crossing

Motion along the spin axis takes the axis explicitly:

along_axis = kerr_geo_capture_axis_infall(0.5, 1.2; axis = :north)
(along_axis.CaseId, along_axis.Trajectory.theta(along_axis.Domain.mino[1] / 2))
(:C3, 0.0)

A scattered orbit

kg = kerr_geodesic(0.5, (1.1, 5.0, 1.0))
scatter = kg.Scatter
(kg.Status.case_ids, scatter.Domain.mino, scatter.ReferenceZero.t_phi_zero_radius)
((:B6, :D2), (-0.5117844324554692, 0.5117844324554692), 5.836447159117277)

The orbit turns at closest approach at $\lambda = 0$; each radius is reached once on the way in and once on the way out. The asymptotic data give the directions of approach and escape and the deflection:

asymptotics = kerr_geo_scatter_asymptotic_diagnostics(scatter)
(asymptotics.azimuthal_deflection.value, asymptotics.deflection_angle_3d.value,
 asymptotics.impact_magnitude.value)
(5.342608514524162, 2.2155999607315984, 11.127293380523735)

A trapped orbit

With $E < 0$ the orbit lives inside the ergoregion. It leaves the past horizon at $-\lambda_H$, turns at $\lambda = 0$ and crosses the future horizon at $\lambda_H$:

trapped = kerr_geo_trapped(0.9, -0.8, -4.0, 1.0)
ΛH = trapped.Domain.horizon_half_duration
(trapped.CaseId, trapped.Domain.mino, trapped.Trajectory.full(0.0).r)
(:N4, (-0.13307897043542027, 0.13307897043542027), 1.5242844915517115)

$t$ and $\phi$ diverge on both horizons. The outgoing coordinates $u$, $\chi$ vanish on the past horizon and the ingoing $v$, $\psi$ on the future one:

(trapped.Trajectory.u(-ΛH), trapped.Trajectory.v(ΛH))
(0.0, 0.0)

component = :incoming keeps the half after the turning point; in kerr_geodesic the keyword is trapped_component:

incoming = kerr_geodesic(0.9, (-0.8, -4.0, 1.0); trapped_component = :incoming).Trapped
incoming.Domain.mino
(0.0, 0.13307897043542027)

Horizon and extremal members

Constants with $P(r_+) = 0$ make the horizon a root of $R$:

a = 0.9
rplus = 1 + sqrt(1 - a^2)
island = kerr_geodesic(a, (0.94, 2rplus * 0.94 / a, 0.0)).Stable
(island.CaseId, island.Tier, kerr_geo_case_name(island.CaseId))
(:A_H1, :horizon, "A-H1")

At $a = \pm1$ every member belongs to the extremal tier. Reflecting the spin and $L_z$ leaves $r$ unchanged and reverses $\phi$:

plus = kerr_geodesic(1.0, (1.2, 2.0, 14.0)).Scatter
minus = kerr_geodesic(-1.0, (1.2, -2.0, 14.0)).Scatter
λ = 0.2 * plus.Domain.mino[2]
(plus.Tier, plus.Trajectory.r(λ) - minus.Trajectory.r(λ),
 plus.Trajectory.phi(λ) + minus.Trajectory.phi(λ))
(:extremal, 0.0, 0.0)

With $P(r_+) = 2E - aL_z = 0$ at $a = 1$ the horizon is a double or triple root, and the members have their own IDs:

kerr_geodesic(1.0, (0.8, 1.6, 1.0)).Stable.CaseId
:A_X1
